# Illustrative Example — Not a Real Study or Evidence

This example shows how the templates can be filled for a hypothetical community service-request information system. It is not a claim about an actual barangay, organization, participant group, or research finding.

## Working title

Design and Evaluation of a Community Service-Request Tracking System in a Local Administrative Setting

## Practical problem (hypothetical)

A local office may receive service requests through multiple informal channels. Requests could be difficult to consolidate, assign, track, and report. Before accepting this as the actual problem, the research team would need interviews, workflow observation, existing records, or other authorized evidence.

## Candidate research gap (provisional)

The study might examine whether a locally adapted request-tracking workflow can meet documented administrative and user requirements, and how its usability and functional suitability perform under a defined evaluation. This is not a novelty claim. A deep literature and comparable-system search is needed first.

## Candidate questions

1. What are the current request-submission, assignment, tracking, and closure workflows and their documented constraints?
2. What functional and non-functional requirements do authorized stakeholders identify for a proposed system?
3. To what extent does the implemented prototype satisfy the specified requirements and usability criteria under a defined test protocol?

## Candidate design

A phased design-and-evaluation study could combine workflow interviews/document analysis, requirements engineering, iterative prototyping, requirements-based tests, and user-centered usability evaluation. The final design must be checked against access, participant availability, ethics review, and adviser requirements.

## What must not be assumed

- That delays or lost requests already occur.
- That all residents have smartphones or reliable connectivity.
- That a particular sample size is adequate.
- That a prototype improves real service outcomes.
- That the office has approved data access or deployment.
- That all personal or incident information may be stored in the proposed system.

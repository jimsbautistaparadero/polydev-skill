# Research Quality Gates and Final Audit

Use these gates at the end of each major phase and before submission. A gate is `pass`, `pass with limitation`, `fail`, or `not yet assessable`. Record evidence for each result.

## Gate 1 — Problem and scope

- [ ] Problem is specific, contextualized, and supported by credible evidence.
- [ ] Practical problem and research gap are distinguished.
- [ ] Aim, scope, unit of analysis, population/system, and exclusions are explicit.
- [ ] Feasibility and access are assessed.
- [ ] Claims of novelty are bounded and supported.

## Gate 2 — Literature and evidence

- [ ] Search strategy covers relevant concepts, terminology, and source venues.
- [ ] Search dates, queries, filters, and selection rules are documented to the required level.
- [ ] Important seminal, recent, local/contextual, method, and contrary sources were considered.
- [ ] Citation metadata and DOI/URLs were verified.
- [ ] Abstract-only and full-text evidence are distinguished.
- [ ] Synthesis compares findings and limitations rather than listing summaries.
- [ ] Gap statement follows from the reviewed evidence and search boundaries.

## Gate 3 — Research architecture

- [ ] Objectives are observable and achievable.
- [ ] Questions follow from objectives and are answerable.
- [ ] Hypotheses are included only when appropriate.
- [ ] Framework constructs and relationships are defined and sourced.
- [ ] Each question maps to evidence, instrument, and analysis.

## Gate 4 — Method and ethics

- [ ] Design is appropriate and justified.
- [ ] Population, eligibility, sampling frame, and sampling procedure are clear.
- [ ] Sample-size or adequacy rationale is provided.
- [ ] Instruments map to constructs/questions and have appropriate review/pilot/validation.
- [ ] Collection steps are reproducible.
- [ ] Analysis is chosen before data collection and assumptions are addressed.
- [ ] Required permissions and ethics approvals are obtained before restricted activities.
- [ ] Privacy, consent, retention, and access controls are documented.

## Gate 5 — Development and evaluation (if applicable)

- [ ] Stakeholder needs have evidence and traceable requirements.
- [ ] Requirements have acceptance criteria.
- [ ] Design decisions and constraints are documented.
- [ ] Test cases map to requirements and evaluation objectives.
- [ ] Actual test conditions and results are recorded.
- [ ] Evaluation measures fit the claims being made.
- [ ] Security, privacy, accessibility, reliability, and failure handling are considered where relevant.

## Gate 6 — Data and analysis

- [ ] Data provenance and authorization are documented.
- [ ] Raw data are preserved and sensitive identifiers protected.
- [ ] Data dictionary, cleaning decisions, and exclusions are documented.
- [ ] Analysis matches the questions, design, measurement, and assumptions.
- [ ] Results are reproducible or the procedure is sufficiently documented.
- [ ] No fabricated, synthetic, or illustrative data are represented as real findings.
- [ ] Interpretation accounts for bias, uncertainty, and limitations.

## Gate 7 — Writing and references

- [ ] Chapter structure matches the institutional manual.
- [ ] Every major claim is supported or explicitly identified as a design decision/result.
- [ ] Every in-text citation matches a reference entry and vice versa.
- [ ] References are verified and formatted consistently.
- [ ] Results and conclusions reflect actual evidence.
- [ ] Limitations are explicit; recommendations follow from findings.
- [ ] Tables, figures, appendices, and cross-references are correct.

## Gate 8 — Defense and handoff

- [ ] Presentation follows the study's actual logic and results.
- [ ] Major choices have defensible rationales.
- [ ] Likely examiner questions have evidence-backed answers.
- [ ] Weaknesses and limits are understood and not concealed.
- [ ] Final documents, instruments, approvals, code/test evidence, and data records are organized.
- [ ] Outstanding issues are listed; completion status is not overstated.

## Critical failure conditions

Do not mark a study ready for submission if any of these remain unresolved:

- Fabricated or unverifiable sources are used as evidence.
- Results or participant data were invented or materially altered.
- Required ethics/permissions were bypassed.
- Research questions cannot be answered by collected evidence.
- Major conclusions exceed the design or results.
- Critical citations, data, or system test results cannot be traced.
